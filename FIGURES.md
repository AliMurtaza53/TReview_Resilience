# Figure storyboard for a Transport Reviews submission

Planned analytical products, not completed findings. Every panel needs a data-source caption, time range, unit/denominator and missingness note. Final scientific figures should be exported as vector PDF/SVG and high-resolution PNG; an interactive companion can expose the same underlying records.

## Figure 1: Selection and scope

PRISMA flow diagram for the completed search. Keep database and supplementary discovery routes identifiable. Distinguish records, reports and underlying studies; show reports not retrieved separately from scientifically excluded studies. No invented pilot counts in a final flowchart. Pair with a small scope diagram for primary studies, prior reviews and contextual asset/sector evidence.

## Figure 2: What earlier reviews cover and share

Panel A: review-by-dimension heatmap, retaining full taxonomy names in supplement. Colors distinguish systematically coded, discussed, outside scope, and unassessed/NR. Panel B: pairwise included-study Jaccard heatmap, with shared counts and directed-containment supplement. Panel C: UpSet intersections or a small review network with edges based only on eligible included studies. Show unknown rosters as missing, not zero overlap. Stratify by freight/passenger/road/multimodal scope and overlapping search windows. Bibliographic coupling belongs in a separately labeled panel, if included at all.

Do not plot a numerical evidence-overlap network until at least two sufficiently complete inclusion rosters have been extracted and normalized. The current eight-review pilot can support a preliminary dimension heatmap; it cannot yet support reliable overlap estimates.

## Figure 3: Research production versus case geography

Two maps, side by side: author-affiliation countries and empirical/model-case countries. Fractional authorship allocation sums to one per paper; also retain full counts in sensitivity checks. Case locations are multi-valued: report both raw case counts and fractional paper allocation. Separate stylized networks, global studies and unspecified cases; never assign Sioux Falls test networks automatically to empirical US evidence. Historical event location is distinct from author affiliation, study-network location and data provenance. Record geocoding source, coordinate precision and country harmonization, not an author's personal nationality. Normalize counts only with a defensible denominator and disclose database/language bias. Do not imply where resilience is greatest.

## Figure 4: Topic and method associations

Use title/abstract/keyword text or standardized bibliography exports for co-word/bibliographic-coupling maps. Define synonym merges and stopword/thesaurus rules (e.g. resilience versus vulnerability; traffic assignment versus decision optimization). State counting scheme, similarity normalization, minimum frequency, edge threshold, community algorithm, seed and sensitivity settings. Inspect cluster labels against full texts; label clusters substantively rather than by software-assigned numbers. Retain peripheral/isolated studies; frequency thresholds can hide new or geographically rare work. A network layout does not provide a meaningful distance scale or causal direction.

## Figure 5: Evidence across the four themes

Evidence map with hazard representation on one axis and flow-model fidelity on the other; facet by temporal treatment or consequence boundary. Bubble area encodes unique eligible study families, not papers plus duplicates. Distinguish observed flows, unweighted routes, demand-loaded shortest paths, congestion-sensitive assignment and simulation. Separate depth/damage realism from traffic-model fidelity. Show cost boundaries and validation as separate encodings/panels rather than a single 'advanced model' score. Use a missingness panel before interpreting sparse combinations.

## Figure 6: Optimization decision map

Alluvial or matrix diagram linking decision stage -> decision variables -> objective -> model family -> validation evidence. Examples include hardening, route/service allocation, repair sequencing and crew logistics. Keep inner traffic assignment distinct from intervention optimization. Multi-label studies need an explicit counting rule: one row per decision problem or fractional contributions summing to one per study. Provide exact counts and avoid implying that more complex algorithms yield more valid recommendations.

## Figure 7: Which evidence supports which claim?

Decision guide linking research question to minimum hazard/flow/time/cost inputs, uncertainty checks, validation and permissible conclusions. This is a synthesis product derived from coded evidence and adjudicated critical assessment, not an algorithm leaderboard. Example boundary: user-delay modeling does not establish GDP loss; optimal routing without compliant behavior does not establish attainable welfare; a solver bound does not validate demand or repair assumptions.

## Figure 8 (optional): Evolution and recurring gaps

Timeline of primary-study design combinations and preceding-review recommendations. Use actual publication and search cutoff dates. Analyze the systematically searched 2019+ primary stratum separately from selectively retained historical studies. Distinguish a gap first identified by a review from one still unresolved in newer eligible work; do not claim a newly discovered gap solely because it appears in the current corpus.

## Suggested main-text selection

Prioritize Figures 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 for the intellectual argument. Figure 1 is methodological reporting; Figure 4 is useful if clustering reveals an interpretable divide; Figure 8 is supplementary unless temporal evidence supports a clear finding. Final panel count and journal format should guide consolidation.
